\section{Introduction}
Since the introduction of GPT-3, the use of large language models (LLMs) beyond niche applications and scientific contexts has grown significantly.
While direct interaction with LLM-based chatbots remains the predominant use case for most individual users, LLMs are increasingly used as the backbone of a broader range of applications.
Examples of LLM integrations include fully or partially automated data processing in applications, agentic models controlling systems independently, and the use of LLMs as reasoning foundations in robotics.\\

On the one hand, a deeper integration of LLMs into applications and processes allows for additional automation and a more effective use of language models' expanding capabilities.
On the other hand, deep integrations separate the model's output further from direct human supervision.
At the same time effects and consequences of model decisions grow as they are integrated into new contexts.
Instead of being limited to text processing and generation directly used by humans, LLMs now influence automated decision-making processes of varying impact and can have direct effects on the physical world when used in robotics or cyber-physical systems.
This trend makes LLMs an increasingly attractive target for attacks.
Both due to the prospect of taking direct control over LLM-integrated systems or due to financial incentives attached to the systems' outputs.
As with any technology that is a potential target for attackers, robustness against attacks is an important requirement for LLMs.\\

In this paper, we demonstrate that the current robustness of modern LLMs does not provide the necessary prerequisites to deeply integrate LLMs into critical or sensitive systems.
We do so by giving an overview of the scientific development of prompt injection attacks against LLMs from late 2022 up to today.
Starting from manual attack prompt construction in~\cref{add_later}, we present early automation approaches in~\cref{add_later}, a formalization and systematization of prompt injection in~\cref{add_later}, and modern gradient-based techniques in~\cref{add_later}.
Throughout this development, we highlight the persistent lack of robustness of state-of-the-art LLMs against the respective most advanced attacks.
Finally, we explore some proposed defense techniques against prompt injection attacks in~\cref{add_later} and discuss conceptual difficulties of making LLMs robust against prompt injection.
